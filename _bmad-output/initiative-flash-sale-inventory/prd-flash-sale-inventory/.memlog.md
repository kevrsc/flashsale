- (decision) Initiative named Flash-sale inventory; folder initiative-flash-sale-inventory set active so the PRD has a home.
- (decision) User wants an inventory service that stays correct when many thousands of people try to buy a limited quantity of one item that just went on sale or was released, using a pattern similar to current retail practice, and asked for recommendations and feedback.
- (event) Landscape research saved to addendum.md as notes only; not adopted as requirements. Open questions held for elicitation: unacceptable miss, fairness rule, and when a unit becomes unavailable.
- (decision) Stakes are a hobby project that may be used for a future launch if the approach proves effective; keep the PRD lean.
- (decision) In scope: a landing page where people request the item, and inventory reservation for the earliest requesters while stock remains. Order processing and related functions are separate services.
- (decision) User stated a quantity limit of 3, and that the same person can try to buy again but will not be able to for 1 hour into the flash sale. Exact meaning of both rules is not yet confirmed.
- (decision) On a successful reservation, a Kafka client writes the reservation to the database and posts to a Kafka topic so other services can continue the order. Mechanism recorded in addendum.md, not as a PRD requirement.
- (decision) User chose the coaching path. Entry point is Vision + Features, as suggested, because the product is the reservation capability and the landing page is how a person reaches it.
- (decision) Vision: limited inventory needs a fair, repeatable standard process so as many interested buyers as possible can reserve and purchase. The process must also be as robust as possible.
- (decision) Robust means nobody is reserved a unit that was not actually available, and the service's own resources handle a high volume of requests. Database contention must be considered; the approach stays in addendum.md.
- (decision) Vision section written to the draft from the confirmed fairness, reservation, and robustness statements.
- (decision) Target user is the builder, ensuring the service works as intended. Practical job: reservations for an item continue if and only if that item's inventory pool is greater than zero.
- (decision) UJ-1: while the pool is above zero, a buyer orders, the service deducts inventory and sends an order event, and the buyer sees that the order is in process. A repeat attempt is told to wait one hour before ordering again.
- (decision) UJ-2: when the inventory pool is not above zero, a later buyer is told the item has sold out. Nothing is deducted and no order event is sent.
- (change) The one-hour rule is a wait before ordering again, not a block that lasts only for the first hour of the sale. The earlier open reading is closed.
- (decision) PRD vocabulary is order, not reserve. Vision, jobs, journeys, and a glossary were updated to match.
- (decision) The customer limit of 3 is the total number of units ordered successfully by a single customer. How many units one order may include is still open.
- (decision) A single order can include more than one unit. The customer limit of 3 can be met in one order or across two or three orders. Feature section FR-1 through FR-4 written from the confirmed journeys, with response gaps left as notes.
- (decision) When an order cannot be accepted in full while the pool is above zero and the customer is not in the one-hour wait, the whole order is refused. No partial acceptance, no deduction, and no order event. The customer sees that the order is refused. FR-5 added.
- (decision) When the inventory pool is empty, the customer sees sold out, including when that customer is also in the one-hour wait. The open response-priority note is closed.
- (decision) Non-goals drafted from existing scope for confirmation: no payment, fulfillment, or completing the purchase; no partial acceptance; order processing does not decide the inventory pool.
- (change) A short inventory pool no longer refuses the whole order. If the customer asks for more units than the pool holds and at least one unit remains, the order succeeds for the units the pool holds so the customer keeps their place. Whole-order refusal remains when the pool could cover the request but it would pass the customer limit. Assumption: a partial order is also capped by the units left under the customer limit.
- (change) The customer limit caps a short-pool partial. When the pool could cover the full request but the order would pass the customer limit, the order is accepted for the units the customer has left, replacing whole-order refusal for that case. A customer with no units left is still refused.
- (decision) User confirmed the draft: a customer with no units left under the customer limit sees that the order is refused, and the non-goal list is complete.
- (decision) MVP is the full behavior already specified. Nothing in the features is deferred. Out of MVP: payment, fulfillment, completing the purchase, and order processing deciding the inventory pool.
- (decision) Success is visibility of traffic and resource usage: logging shows total requests and how many database queries and updates occurred. Counter-metric: those counts are not success if a deducted unit was unavailable or a customer exceeds the limit of 3.
- (decision) A customer has an account. The service recognizes the customer by that account's user ID. Nothing else is unresolved. Account creation and management are treated as outside this service.

- (event) Polish applied structure then prose: removed the working-title prompt and coaching asides, pointed MVP at the feature and non-goal sections, and left requirements in place.
