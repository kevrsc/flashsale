- (note) Addendum landscape patterns and the three elicitation questions were not adopted. The final PRD already resolved fairness as earliest successful orders, unavailability at deduction, and no payment in this service.
- (capability) CAP-1 Place an order the pool and the limit can cover. Intent: a customer can order one or more units when the pool holds at least those units, the order would not pass the customer limit, and the customer is not in the one-hour wait. Success: those units are deducted, an order event is sent for them, the customer sees the order is in process, and the units are not available to another customer.
- (capability) CAP-2 Report sold out. Intent: a customer who orders when the inventory pool is not above zero sees that the item has sold out. Success: nothing is deducted, no order event is sent, and sold out is shown even when that customer is also in the one-hour wait.
- (capability) CAP-3 Enforce the one-hour wait. Intent: after a successful order, that customer must wait one hour before ordering again while the pool is above zero. Success: an order during the wait is not successful, the customer is told to wait one hour, and nothing is deducted and no order event is sent.
- (capability) CAP-4 Enforce the customer limit. Intent: a customer successful orders total at most 3 units, in one order or across two or three orders. Success: no successful order leaves that customer above 3 units. One order of 3 can meet the limit. Two or three successful orders can meet it when their units add to 3, with the one-hour wait after each.
- (capability) CAP-5 Accept up to the limit. Intent: when the pool could cover the full order and the order asks for more than the customer has left, the order succeeds for the units left, when that number is at least one. Success: those units are deducted, the event is for the units deducted, the customer sees the order is in process, and the total does not exceed 3. When none are left, the order is refused.
- (capability) CAP-6 Accept a short pool. Intent: when the pool holds at least one unit and the order asks for more than the pool holds, the order succeeds for a subset and the customer keeps that place. Success: units deducted are the smaller of units asked, units the pool holds, and units left under the limit. The event is for the units deducted. The customer sees the order is in process. When none are left under the limit, the order is refused as in CAP-5.
- (capability) CAP-7 Show sale counts. Intent: the builder can see how many order attempts arrived and how many database queries and updates ran. Success: those totals are visible for a sale, and the sale is not a success when a deducted unit was not in the pool or a customer exceeds 3 successful units.
- (capability) CAP-8 Reject a blank attempt. Intent: a blank user id or a non-positive quantity is not an order. Success: nothing is deducted, no order event is sent, and the customer sees that the order was not accepted.
- (constraint) The deduction and the order event are one outcome. An event is not sent for units that were not deducted, and a deduction does not stand without the event.
- (constraint) The landing page is part of this service. It collects the user id and the quantity. It does not decide the inventory pool.
- (constraint) The customer is recognized only by the user ID of an account this service does not create, manage, or authenticate.
- (constraint) There is one item. Its inventory pool is the only count of units still available.
- (constraint) Order processing continues from the order event and does not decide whether the pool allows another order.
- (constraint) The service handles a high volume of order attempts, including contention for the shared pool.
- (constraint) Request totals and database query and update totals are visible in the service log. A metrics endpoint does not replace that log.
- (constraint) Units go to the earliest successful orders.
- (constraint) Consistency of the page, the inventory decision, publication, and the local runtime is governed by the architecture spine.
- (decision) Non-goals: payment, fulfillment, and completing the purchase; creating or managing accounts; a second item; a hosted environment; restock after the pool hits zero; Avro, Protobuf, or a schema registry.
- (decision) Success signal: on the local service, a sale shows orders succeeding only while the pool is above zero, no unit deducted twice, no customer past 3, the five shopper outcomes matching the rules, an order event for the units deducted, and log totals for requests and database statements.
